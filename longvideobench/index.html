LongVideoBench

Referring reasoning over long web videos with interleaved subtitles: a referring query locates a moment, the question asks about it. 17 categories at two levels (perception, relation), four duration groups up to one hour.

Statistics

  • 3,761 web videos with subtitles, 8 s to 59:34 long (median 8:41).
  • 6,678 multiple-choice questions: 1,337 (validation) come with answers, 5,341 (test) do not; the examples below are from validation. Each question first describes a moment, then asks about it.

Construction

Figure 1. Videos and task design meet in annotation, then evaluation
Paper: Sec. 2, Sec. 3, Sec. 4.1, App. A.2

Read the top row first: two independent inputs. On the left are the videos, downloaded from 119 channels with subtitles attached, in four duration groups from 8 s to one hour. On the right is the task design: every question must open with a referring query that names a moment of the video and then ask about that moment, in one of 17 categories. Both arrive at the annotators, who write questions following per-category instructions and check one another's work.

The running example enters at the collection stage as a travel vlog, becomes the backpack question during annotation (a referring query about a woman with a red top and a black backpack, two labelled moments, four options) and leaves as one of 6,678 questions. At evaluation time the model under test receives the sampled frames with the subtitles interleaved, then the question, and has to answer with a single option letter.

The two colours separate what the model sees (purple) from what only the annotation side has (gold). The frame indices of the referred moments are annotated but never shown to the model; they serve the authors' analysis of where in the video the queried moment lies. Figure 2 opens the collection box.

Figure 2. Video and subtitle collection: from 119 channels to 3,763 videos
Paper: Sec. 3.1, Sec. 3.2, Fig. 3, Tab. 4

Follow the flow from the top. Every video with at least 720p resolution is downloaded from the 119 channels, about 55.8K in total. The fork below handles subtitles: when the platform provides a transcript, the video is kept only if that transcript is in English; when it provides none, Whisper-V3-Large produces one. Both branches are then sampled so that topics are evenly covered and scored with Q-Align; videos below 0.25 on its 0 to 1 scale are dropped.

The last filter is human: the primary annotator (gold) discards videos that cannot carry a referring question, such as still frames or incomplete subtitles. What remains are the 3,763 videos, each with time-stamped subtitles. The running example's travel vlog, shot in landscape, passes through all of these steps; the paper does not say which subtitle branch it took.

The sampling step is where the raw channel counts collapse: news channels yielded 24,002 downloads but only 329 annotated videos. Figure 3 shows how the result is spread over durations and categories.

Figure 3. Four duration groups and ten category groups
Paper: Sec. 3.1, Tab. 3, Tab. 4

The upper group lists the four duration groups. The two short groups mix landscape and portrait videos and average 11 to 12 s and 34 to 39 s; the two long groups are landscape only and average 6.5 and 23.5 minutes. The per-group counts (884, 925, 986, 966) are taken from Table 3; they sum to 3,761, two short of the 3,763 stated in the text.

The lower group gives the ten categories: movie recaps, three life categories, news, and five knowledge categories, each with its number of source channels and annotated videos per layout. Knowledge channels are landscape only, and STEM and computer science ended up with fewer videos than the other categories.

The running example's video is a landscape travel vlog; the paper states neither its duration group nor its category, so the Travel guides label in the last row is a guess. Figure 4 turns to what a question on such a video looks like.

Figure 4. One referring reasoning question: query, referred context, body, options
Paper: Sec. 2, Fig. 1, Sec. 3.3

Read top to bottom. A question is built from a referring query, which describes one or several moments of the video, and a question body, which asks something about those moments; the answer is one of four or five options. In the running example the query is the woman with the headband, the red top and the black backpack at the beginning of the video; the referred context is that opening scene together with the moment she comes down the rocky hill; the body asks what changed on her backpack.

The two levels at the bottom are defined by how many moments the query points to. Perception (L1) questions refer to one moment and ask what is in it; relation (L2) questions refer to several moments and ask about their order, a change between them, or which other moments contain the same object. The backpack question compares two moments, so it is a relation question about an attribute change.

The design point is that the model has to find the referred context from the query alone: the frames carry no markers, so the query must be specific enough to be unambiguous, which is why a question averages 43.5 words. Figure 5 lists the 17 categories.

Figure 5. The 17 categories, by referring query and target answer
Paper: Sec. 2, Tab. 2, Fig. 2

Each row is one category, named as in the paper's Table 2, with the code used on the leaderboard and a short formula: what the referring query describes, then what the answer is about. The query can describe a scene, an event or an object seen in the frames, or quote a subtitle (the text-referred categories), sometimes combined with an object; the answer is usually an event, an object or an attribute.

Two categories ask for something other than a visual concept. Sequence of scenes (SSS) asks for the order of at least three scenes, and its distractors are permutations of the correct order; text-referred object tracking (TOS) asks which subtitle was on screen when the object appeared. The running example, two scenes plus a backpack and a question about what changed, matches scene-referred object attribute change (SAA); this assignment is ours, the paper does not label its Fig. 1 example.

There are 3,204 perception and 3,474 relation questions, between 348 and 410 per category. Figure 6 shows how annotators produced them.

Figure 6. Human annotation: training, interface fields, examiner, reviser
Paper: Sec. 3.3, App. E.1, App. E.2, Fig. 6

Start at the top. Annotators were trained with one instruction sheet per category, each a short recipe with example questions (the sheets are reproduced in prompts.json). The primary annotator watches the whole video with the subtitles displayed under the frames, may then seek back freely, and writes one question for a video up to 60 s, two for 3 to 10 min and three for 15 to 60 min; videos that cannot be annotated are dropped at this point.

The dashed group lists what one annotation contains, following the fields of the interface: the question, the referring query marked as a part of it, the frame numbers of the referred moments (one for L1, two or more for L2), four or five candidate options, the correct one, and a confirmation that the referred moment is really needed to answer. For the backpack question that means the query about the woman, two frame numbers and four options.

Every video then passes an examiner, who checks that the question belongs to its category and meets all requirements, and a reviser, who rewrites what was flagged; about a fifth of the annotations were revised. The work took about 1,500 annotator hours. Figure 7 shows how a finished question is put to a model.

Figure 7. Evaluation: sampled frames, interleaved subtitles, question, one letter back
Paper: Sec. 3.2, Sec. 4.1, App. A.2, official data loader

The protocol follows the official data loader. Frames are sampled evenly over the video, at most one per second and never more than max_frames; a 10 s clip therefore always yields 10 frames, while a 40 min video yields max_frames (the authors go up to 256 for the proprietary models). Each subtitle line is then inserted into the frame list at its midpoint in time, between the last frame before it and the first frame after it; a line whose span contains no sampled frame is left out.

The dashed group is the sequence the model receives (purple): the interleaved list, then 'Question:' followed by the full question text starting with the running example's referring query, the options lettered A to E, and a fixed instruction to answer with the letter only. The reply is compared with the correct choice; no judge model is involved.

Accuracy is reported per duration group and per category, on a validation subset (752 videos, 1,337 questions, answers released) and a test subset (3,011 videos, 5,341 questions, answers withheld for the leaderboard). The same loader provides the ablation inputs of the paper's Table 6, subtitles only and frames only, plus a question-only mode.

Annotation instruction I: SCENE-REFERRED EVENT (S2E) App. E.1; Step-by-step instruction given to human annotators for writing S2E questions, with example questions.
I. SCENE-REFERRED EVENT (S2E)
1. Find an action or event.
2. Pause, describe/outline the scene information as the question stem.
3. Use this action or event as the answer.
4. You may refer to these examples:
• What is the boy in the video doing at Danube Square?
• What happens after all the ingredients are placed in the pot?
• When the video transitions to the office, what are the employees doing?
• What are the characters in the video doing in the café? (non-knowledge videos)
• What was George Washington doing under the apple tree? (History)
• What happens in the experiment verifying the surface tension of oil? (Physics)
Annotation instruction II: SCENE-REFERRED OBJECT (S2O) App. E.1; Step-by-step instruction given to human annotators for writing S2O questions, with example questions.
II. SCENE-REFERRED OBJECT (S2O)
1. Find a scene, observe the people or objects in this scene.
2. Describe/outline the scene information as the question stem.
3. Use the appearing people/objects and the absent ones as correct and incorrect answers respectively.
4. You may refer to these examples:
• What objects appeared in Laura's bedroom in the video? (Lifestyle)
• When all the ingredients are chopped and placed together, which ingredient did not appear? (Cooking)
• Which communication method was not mentioned in the fourth section? (Physics)
• Which character did not appear at the duel in the movie?

III. SCENE-REFERRED OBJECT ATTRIBUTE (S2A)
1. Find a scene, observe the people or objects in this scene.
2. Describe/outline the scene information and determine an object as the question stem.
3. Use existing and non-existing attributes of the object as correct and incorrect answers respectively, such as material, color, shape, transparency, surface characteristics, structural features.
4. You may refer to these examples:
• What clothes is Laura wearing in the bedroom with an air conditioner, a bed, and a clothes rack?
• What color is used to represent the feed forward layer in the Transformer network in Figure 4?

Annotation instruction IV: EVENT-REFERRED OBJECT (E2O) App. E.1; Step-by-step instruction given to human annotators for writing E2O questions, with example questions.
IV. EVENT-REFERRED OBJECT (E2O)
1. Find an action or event.
2. Identify the participating people or objects.
3. Describe this action/event as the question stem.
4. Based on the subtitles at the time of the action/event or other background information, detail the participating people/objects as the answer. The options should also be as detailed as possible.
5. You may refer to these examples:
• Who participated in and won the duel in the movie?
• Which character finished knitting the sweater?
• What object exploded in the chemistry experiment in the video?

Annotation instruction V: OBJECT-REFERRED EVENT (O2E) App. E.1; Step-by-step instruction given to human annotators for writing O2E questions, with example questions.
V. OBJECT-REFERRED EVENT (O2E)
1. Find a person or object.
2. Identify the actions/events that happens at their appearance.
3. Describe the person/object as the question stem.
4. Based on a scene where this person/object appears (e.g., first appearance), ask what event happened or what action they took at that time.
5. You may refer to these examples:

Annotation instruction VI: TEXT-REFERRED EVENT (T2E) App. E.1; Step-by-step instruction given to human annotators for writing T2E questions, with example questions.
VI. TEXT-REFERRED EVENT (T2E)
1. Find a segment of subtitles, pause the video.
2. Identify the action in the current frame of the video.
3. Think of a few actions that did not appear in the video but are easily confused.
4. Use the action from step 2 as the correct answer, and the actions from step 3 as other options.
5. You may refer to these examples:
• What was the protagonist doing when mentioning the Renaissance?
• What event happened when "bidirectional encoder" first appeared in the subtitles?
Annotation instruction VII: TEXT-REFERRED OBJECT (T2O) App. E.1; Step-by-step instruction given to human annotators for writing T2O questions, with example questions.
VII. TEXT-REFERRED OBJECT (T2O)
1. Find a segment of subtitles, pause the video.
2. Identify a certain object in the frame; for example, a black water bottle.
3. Think of a few objects that did not appear in the video but are easily confused, such as a red water bottle, a black hat, a water dispenser, a transparent water cup.
4. Use the object from step 2 as the correct answer, and the objects from step 3 as other options.
5. You may refer to these examples:
• What object was present when the lecturer mentioned "revolutionary changes"?
• Which object did not appear when talking about Jack and Rose having a heart-to-heart conversation?

VIII. TEXT-REFERRED OBJECT ATTRIBUTE (S2A)

2. Identify a certain object in the frame.
3. Identify an attribute of the object, such as material, color, shape, transparency, surface characteristics, structural features.

IX. EVENT BEFORE/AFTER EVENT (E3E)
1. Find two or more adjacent actions or events.
2. Describe one of the actions/events as the question stem, and the other as the correct answer.

X. OBJECT BEFORE/AFTER OBJECT (O3O)
1. Find two or more people/objects/concepts that appear in the video.
2. Describe one of the objects as the question stem, and the other as the correct answer.
3. You may refer to these examples:

Annotation instruction XI: SEQUENCE OF SCENES (SSS) App. E.1; Step-by-step instruction given to human annotators for writing SSS questions, with example questions.
XI. SEQUENCE OF SCENES (SSS)
1. Find multiple scenes (at least three) in the video.
2. Ask questions about the order of these scenes.
3. Answer with the correct sequence and use a few scrambled sequences as distractors.
4. You may refer to this example:
• Which of the following scene sequences is correct?
• A. First, a segment of the experiment video is played, then slides with text are shown, and finally XXXX.
• B. First, slides with text are shown, ...
Annotation instruction XII: SCENE-REFERRED OBJECT TRACKING (SOS) App. E.1; Step-by-step instruction given to human annotators for writing SOS questions, with example questions.
XII. SCENE-REFERRED OBJECT TRACKING (SOS)
1. Find a specific person/object/concept that appears in multiple scenes.
2. Define this person/object/concept by their action/attribute in one of the scenes.
3. Then ask in which other scenes did they appear.
4. Distractors are scenes where this object did not appear.
5. You may refer to these examples:
• In which of the following places did the boy who was running at the beginning of the video appear?
– A. Square on a sunny day,
– B. On a boat at sea,
– C. In a bar on a rainy day, ...
• In which other scenes did the protagonist's lightsaber, used in the opening fight, appear?
Annotation instruction XIII: SCENE-REFERRED OBJECT ATTRIBUTE CHANGE (SAA) App. E.1; Step-by-step instruction given to human annotators for writing SAA questions, with example questions.
XIII. SCENE-REFERRED OBJECT ATTRIBUTE CHANGE (SAA)
1. Find a specific person/object/concept that appears in multiple scenes.
2. Define this person/object/concept by their action/attribute in one of the scenes.
3. Then describe another scene and ask what attribute of this person/object/concept has changed at that time.
4. You may refer to these examples:
• What did the boy running at the beginning of the video change into when climbing the mountain at the end?
– A. Changed from a white T-shirt to a black vest
– B. Changed from red shoes to white shoes
– C. ...
• What changed in the color of the onions initially poured into the pot?
• What new part did the sapling planted in the soil at the beginning grow in the later part of the video?
Annotation instruction XIV: EVENT BEFORE/AFTER TEXT (T3E) App. E.1; Step-by-step instruction given to human annotators for writing T3E questions, with example questions.
XIV. EVENT BEFORE/AFTER TEXT (T3E)
1. Find a segment of subtitles, and an action/event in the video that happens before/after it.
2. Rephrase/outline the subtitle as the given information and design the question stem, with the action/event as the correct answer.
3. Distractors are other actions/events in the video that do not meet the sequence relationship in the question stem.
4. You may refer to these examples:
• What did Clara do after she said, "I eat an apple every day"?
• What happened before the narrator mentioned the experiment starting?
• What action was performed after the chef said, "Now wait until the steak surface turns golden"?
Annotation instruction XV: OBJECT BEFORE/AFTER TEXT (T3O) App. E.1; Step-by-step instruction given to human annotators for writing T3O questions, with example questions.
XV. OBJECT BEFORE/AFTER TEXT (T3O)
1. Find the scene where a specific person/object first appears.
2. Then find subtitles before or after this timeframe, rephrase/outline the subtitle as the given information and design the question stem, with the object/person as the correct answer.
3. Distractors are other people/objects in the video that do not meet the sequence relationship in the question stem.
4. You may refer to these examples:
• Which characters appeared after the commentary mentioned "100 years later"?
• Which animal appeared on screen before mentioning "dietary habits of North American squirrels"?
Annotation instruction XVI: TEXT-REFERRED OBJECT TRACKING (TOS) App. E.1; Step-by-step instruction given to human annotators for writing TOS questions, with example questions.
XVI. TEXT-REFERRED OBJECT TRACKING (TOS)
1. Find a specific person/object/concept that appeared at least once along with subtitles.
2. Define this person/object/concept by their action/attribute in one of the scenes.
3. Ask on a subtitle at the object's appearance.

5. You may refer to these examples:
• With which subtitles did the boy running at the beginning of the video appear?
• During which of the following dialogues did the protagonist's lightsaber, used in the opening fight, appear on screen?

Phi-3-Vision chat wrapper App. A.2; Wraps the interleaved list in Phi-3's chat tokens and numbered image placeholders before the model is called.
class Counter:
    def __init__(self):
        self.counter = 0
    def __call__(self):
        self.counter += 1
        return self.counter

user_prompt = "<|user|>"
assistant_prompt = "<|assistant|>"
prompt_suffix = "<|end|>"

def phi3_preprocess(interleaved_list):
    counter = Counter()
    placeholder_list, image_list = [user_prompt], []
    for itm in interleaved_list:
        if isinstance(itm, str):
            placeholder_list.append(itm)
        else:
            placeholder_list.append("<|image_{}|>".format(counter()))
            image_list.append(resize_image(itm))
    placeholder_list.extend([prompt_suffix, assistant_prompt, ""])
    return "\n".join(placeholder_list), image_list
Mantis-BakLLaVA image-token wrapper App. A.2; Replaces each image of the interleaved list with the '<image>' token for Mantis-BakLLaVA.
def mantis_preprocess(interleaved_list):
    placeholder_list, image_list = [], []
    for itm in interleaved_list:
        if isinstance(itm, str):
            placeholder_list.append(itm)
        else:
            placeholder_list.append("<image>")
            image_list.append(itm)
    text = "\n".join(placeholder_list)
    # following official guide, use " " to divide image tokens
    text = text.replace("<image>\n", "<image> ")
    return text, image_list
